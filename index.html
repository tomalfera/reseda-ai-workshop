Proposal: Reseda AI Workshop

A student-led extracurricular club at Reseda Charter High School · Proposed Fall 2026 · Faculty Sponsor: Tom Alfera

🎯 Mission

The Reseda AI Workshop is a build-first, no-pressure space where students learn artificial intelligence by making things — not by sitting through lectures. AI literacy is the defining skill of the next decade, and students learn best when they're in the driver's seat: building their own AI assistants, automating school processes, and creating projects that matter to them. No prerequisites. No grades. No pitch decks. Just a room, some computers, and the willingness to try.

🔧 What We'll Do

"Your Own Agent" Series

Week 1: build a personal AI assistant. Week 2: teach it a skill. Week 3: connect them together. Every kid gets their own Athena.

Reseda High AI

A student-built app answering "when is the pep rally," "how do I appeal a grade." Used by 2,000 kids.

Contract Checker — Student Rights

Like the UTLA contract app, but for the student handbook. Kids learn the tech AND their rights.

AI Art & Music Collective

For kids who don't code: AI-generated covers, album art, drama posters. Low floor, high ceiling.

"Automate the Boring Stuff"

Hunt for tedious school processes and automate them. Permission slips, club attendance, bathroom passes.

🎓 Benefits to Students & School

📁 Portfolios

Every project is a resume line or college app asset.

🤝 Cross-Curricular

English, art, social studies, and STEM — all in one club.

🏫 School-Wide Impact

Projects serve the entire RCHS community of 2,000+ students.

♿ EL & Accessibility

AI tools translate, scaffold, and support diverse learners by design.

🧭 Teacher Support

AI tools assist with lesson planning, differentiation, admin tasks.

💵 Cost: $0

Free tools only. No paid subscriptions, no special equipment.

📅 Structure

  • Meeting cadence: Once weekly, after school (60–90 min)
  • Format: 10-min intro → 50 min self-directed building → optional share-out
  • Location: Classroom with district-issued Chromebooks
  • Membership: Open to all RCHS students. No application, no prerequisites.
  • Cost to school: $0 — browser-based AI tools, open-source frameworks only.

📱

Using Technology With Intention

Response to LAUSD Board Resolution Res-048-25/26 — "Using Technology with Intention: Establishing Guidelines for Student Screen Time"

We agree with the Board. The resolution's title says it all: technology should be used with intention. The Reseda AI Workshop is not more screen time — it is the most intentional, creative, and collaborative use of technology a student can engage in. We are the solution the resolution is asking for.

The Real Problem Isn't Screens. It's Passivity.

The resolution targets a real concern: students spending hours in passive consumption — doom-scrolling, infinite feeds, algorithmically-served content designed to numb rather than engage. That kind of screen time is solitary, consumptive, and mindless.

The Reseda AI Workshop is the opposite on every axis:

❌ Passive Screen Time

  • Scrolling, watching, consuming
  • Algorithm-curated, attention-optimized
  • Solitary — alone with a device
  • Designed to keep you passive
  • No output, no skill, no growth

✅ AI Workshop Screen Time

  • Building, coding, designing, creating
  • Student-driven, curiosity-optimized
  • Collaborative — in a room, working together
  • Designed to produce, share, and iterate
  • Tangible output, real skills, real growth

How the Workshop Embodies "Intentional" Technology Use

🏗️ Creation, Not Consumption

Every minute of screen time produces something: a working AI assistant, an automation script, a piece of art. Students leave with artifacts, not just screen fatigue.

🤝 In-Person Collaboration

This isn't remote or isolated. Students are in a room together — talking, helping, arguing about approaches, laughing at bugs. The screens are tools for conversation, not substitutes for it.

🧭 Purpose-Driven

Students don't "go online" — they arrive with a project and a goal. The screen is a workshop bench, not a lounger. Intentionality is built into the format.

⏱️ Time-Bounded

60–90 minutes, once a week. Structured: 10-minute intro → 50 minutes of focused building → optional share-out. The session ends. Students go home. It's additive, not all-consuming.

📚 Cross-Curricular Growth

Projects connect to English (writing), art (design), social studies (ethics, rights), and STEM (coding, logic). Screen time here reinforces classroom learning, not competes with it.

🛡️ Adult-Facilitated

A credentialed teacher is present at every session — guiding, troubleshooting, and modeling ethical, intentional technology use. This is supervised, structured, educational screen time.

In the Board's Own Words

The resolution is titled "Using Technology with Intention." This workshop is, by design, the most intentional use of educational technology available to students. We don't ask for an exemption from the policy — we argue that the workshop is the policy, fully realized:

  • Intentional? Students arrive with a project goal. Every action is purposeful.
  • Educational? AI literacy, coding, design thinking, collaboration — all in one hour.
  • Supervised? Faculty sponsor present at all times.
  • Time-limited? 60–90 minutes, once weekly. Starts and ends on a clock.
  • Productive? Students leave with something they built. Portfolios grow. Skills compound.

What We're Actually Fighting

A flat-minute screen time policy that doesn't distinguish between:

  • Watching TikTok alone in bed at midnight
  • Building a working AI assistant with friends after school, supervised by a teacher

These are not the same activity. Treating them as identical doesn't protect students — it deprives them of the one kind of screen time that actually prepares them for the world they're inheriting.

The Reseda AI Workshop doesn't ask for an exception to the screen time policy.

We ask to be recognized as what the policy's own title describes: technology used with intention.
